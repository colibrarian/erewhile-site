We designed its library: every presentation, guide and tool from the course, sorted by topic and open to any youth worker.

The task

Make a week of training useful for months

A training course lasts a week. Youth workers need its materials much longer: to run the same activity with their own groups, to check a fact, to remember how a method worked.

Participants and partner organisations from six countries needed one place where everything from the course can be found again, on a phone or a projector, without logins or downloads.

What we built

A library, not a folder of files

Library by topicSix topics, and every material has a cover, a type and a length, so people find it by sight instead of by file name.
Interactive bias map40 cognitive biases around one brain, each marked by how well the research supports it. Click any bias to read an example and the sources.
Slide decks in the browserOne idea per screen, a chapter colour that shows where you are, arrow-key navigation and an overview of all slides. Nothing to install.
One-page guides and memosA fact-checking toolkit and a podcast conversation memo, short enough to print and keep at hand.
Visibility kitA ready caption and partner tags with copy buttons, so every post from the course carries the required Erasmus+ text.
Part of the project siteThe library is page six of the project’s site on untu.ngo, next to the programme, travel and preparation pages.
Visual language

Loud on the shelf, calm inside

The library speaks in tall Anton capitals on dark ink, like the spines on a shelf. Inside, the materials switch to a calmer system for reading on a big screen: Bricolage Grotesque headings, Figtree text and JetBrains Mono labels on light paper. One idea per screen, and colour tells you which chapter you are in.
